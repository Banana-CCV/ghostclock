# C4 progress log (visual / rolling-shutter ENF)

All times are on Sun 8 Nov 2026 and approximate. "REAL" means Zenodo data. "SYNTHETIC" means
generated by `synth_rs.py`.

* **Orient.** Read W04, W08 and W09 outputs, read-only.
  * W04 had cached the Zenodo listing.
  * W08's OpenAlex cache supplied abstracts.
  * No `datasets/raw/gb/` exists, so the synthetic generator uses an OU random-walk ENF instead.
* **Zenodo API** (`zenodo_list.py` → `zenodo_files.json`). 17 files, 21.32 GB in total: 8 MP4
  files of 1.21–3.54 GB and 9 WAV files of 1.8 MB each.
  * Smallest video: `MOV2_20190508_200450A.mp4` (1,212,054,170 B). Matching reference:
    `b827ebd746d7_tensiunator2_20190508_200340.wav`, same date, starting 70 s earlier by
    filename.
  * Both were downloaded to `datasets/raw/video_c4/` and **both md5 checksums match Zenodo**.
* **ffprobe** (`ffprobe_MOV2.json`, `mov2_pts.csv`):
  * Video: H.264 Main, 1280×720, yuv420p tv-range, about 15 Mb/s, 647.43 s, 19,441 frames,
    r_frame_rate 30/1 but avg 30.026 fps.
  * PTS: millisecond-quantised steps of 33/34 ms, plus 57 steps of 31, 32 or 35 ms.
  * A linear fit gives 30.0262 fps with residual RMS 0.30 ms, max 2.1 ms, and no steps (the
    301-frame rolling median of the residual stays within ±0.02 ms). We treat it as a **steady
    clock at 30.026 fps**; the nominal-rate VFR flag is caused by the stated 30/1.
  * Audio: AAC at 8 kHz, near-silent at 375 b/s.
  * Reference WAV: PCM s16, **1000 Hz**, mono, exactly 900 s, mains voltage waveform.
* **Literature.** Full text of Vatansever 2019 (arXiv) read with pypdf, installed in the
  scratchpad only. PMC8538866 is Karantaidis & Kotropoulos 2021, the paper that evaluates this
  database. **It is not a Vatansever paper**; METHOD.md corrects this. par.nsf.gov and MDPI
  refused or 403'd, so Hajj-Ahmad 2016 is cited from metadata and S1's restatement.
* **Real-video decode** to a cell cache (180 row groups × 16 column blocks per frame). About
  6 min at roughly 55–80 fps; the machine was shared with other workers at 100 % CPU.
* **First real run** (old code, ± T_ro search, 60 s high-pass on z):
  * T_ro = 28.20 ms, RS median q 44 dB, naive median q 14 dB.
  * RS correlation with the reference was 0.53 at the nominal 70 s lag and 0.91 at the best lag
    of 82 s.
* **Synthetic run 1** (archived in `synth/old_pm_search_hp/`) exposed two bugs:
  1. **Read-out direction ambiguity.** At 25 fps the ± T_ro search chose −25.25 ms, which
     flipped the ENF sign (r = −0.949). With the true +25 ms, r = +0.948.
     * Cause: when fps divides f0, +T_ro and −T_ro score identically.
     * Fix: assume top-to-bottom read-out (T_ro > 0) by default. Bidirectional search is
       optional and flagged.
  2. **The 60 s high-pass on z hurt.** On SYNTHETIC fps30_tro25 it raised RMSE from
     **0.34 to 3.27 mHz** (r 0.999 → 0.923); measured with `ablation_hp_30fps.py` (and `ablation_scene_removal_25fps.py` for 25 fps). They call the low-level functions with explicit choices, so they reproduce independently of the `run_video` defaults.
     * At 30 fps the scene sits at −10 Hz in baseband and needs no removal.
     * Fix: remove only the whole-clip mean of z, and only at degenerate fps. On fps25
       (oracle T_ro) the ablation gave whole-clip mean r = 0.986 / RMSE 0.97 mHz against 60 s
       moving mean r = 0.948 / RMSE 1.90 mHz.
* **Re-runs** with the fixed code: see RESULTS.json and the README tables.
* **Fixed-code re-runs.** This chapter also added the **strip-wise alias baseline**: per-row-band
  frame-rate sampling with spectra summed incoherently. Without it, the rolling-shutter extractor
  would only have been compared against the weakest baseline.
  * REAL MOV2:
    * The search gives T_ro = 28.25 ms with T_ro > 0 enforced, and 28.20 ms when both
      directions are searched. The mirror direction scores 0.0054, so the direction is
      identified at 30 fps.
    * Rolling-shutter extractor: r = 0.9954 at lag 81.5 s, RMSE 4.48 mHz, bias +4.35 mHz,
      debiased RMSE 1.07 mHz, q 45 dB.
    * Strip-wise baseline: r = 0.9967, q 30.5 dB.
    * Naive whole-frame mean: r = 0.38, q 14 dB.
    * At the filename lag of 70 s the rolling-shutter extractor gives r = 0.645.
    * The full-range lag search (0–252.5 s) gives a unique peak at 81.5 s; the next best more
      than 10 s away is 0.685.
  * SYNTHETIC (7 cases):
    * Rolling-shutter RMSE is 0.27–0.34 mHz at 30 and 60 fps, against 0.67–0.94 for strip-wise
      and 1.62–3.29 for naive.
    * At 25 fps: rolling shutter 0.96 mHz (r 0.986); strip-wise r −0.994 (sign-flipped,
      94 mHz); naive 86 mHz.
    * Anti-banding (10 ms exposure) and weak/CRF 28: every method fails, and q drops to about
      4 dB.
* **Plots.**
  * The banding demo uses the quietest 2 s of the clip, at about 32 s. With frames 3000–3059
    the walking person dominated the residual.
  * The spectrogram uses a 32 s window for visual sharpness only; the metrics use 16 s.
* **Deliverables.** README.md, METHOD.md, FAILURES.md, RESULTS.json and plots/. Nothing was
  pushed or published.

## Not done (time box)

* Only 1 of the 8 Zenodo videos was processed. MOV1 (static) and MOV3 (heavy motion) were not
  tested.
* No real 25 fps clip, so the degenerate-rate advantage is synthetic only.
* There is no q-gate calibration on real negatives.
* Dropped-frame detection and motion-weighted frames are not implemented.
* The Hajj-Ahmad 2016 and Su 2014 full texts were not read (fetch blocked). METHOD.md cites them
  from metadata and from Vatansever 2019's restatement.
