# GhostClock: finance cases where timing, misdating or fabricated media cost money

Compiled 2026-11-08 from a quick web pass of about 15 minutes. Every figure below comes from the cited source. Where sources disagree, the range is given. "Checked" means the page was fetched and the figure confirmed. "Search" means the figure was confirmed only from search-result text from that source; reload it before putting it on a slide.

Fit ratings:
- **DIRECT**: the time or authenticity of a recording is the central question.
- **PARTIAL**: the time or authenticity of media matters, but ENF may not apply (still image, text, phone-band audio, or a live call).
- **ANALOGY-ONLY**: shows that seconds and timestamps carry money, but no recording is involved.

## Corrections to our brief (don't repeat these on stage)
- **Thomson Reuters / U. Michigan data, two seconds early.** This was a **July 2013** agreement with the NY AG, not a 2014 settlement. No penalty amount was found. In 2014 the university moved distribution to Bloomberg, and the AG said this ended the practice permanently.
- **Pentagon AI image.** Reputable outlets give an S&P 500 dip of **0.26% (The Hill/CNN/Politico) to 0.29% (AFP)**, recovered within about 10 minutes. The "$500bn market-cap swing" comes from a Twitter post by The Kobeissi Letter. **No independent confirmation was found; don't use it.**
- **AP tweet, $136bn.** Confirmed as **about $136.5bn of S&P 500 value, per Reuters data** (Reuters, Alina Selyukh).
- **Options backdating "number of companies."** The SEC chairman told the WSJ in 2006 that **more than 80 companies** were under investigation (NPR). CNN Money, July 2006: more than 50 under SEC/DOJ investigation, and probably more than 100 backdated. Later press: "more than 200 implicated" (CFO.com). Use "80+ under SEC investigation."
- **Deepfake CEO video moving a stock.** **No documented case of a share-price drop was found.** NSE (Apr 2024) and BSE (Jan 2026) issued investor warnings about deepfake videos of their CEOs giving stock tips, but no price impact was reported.
- **FINRA clock-sync fines.** No FINRA action about clock sync *alone* was found. CODA Markets (2019) is the closest: clock-sync procedures were missing and timestamps were inaccurate.

## The three best pitch summaries

**1. "Machines trade on media within seconds; nobody checks when or whether it was recorded."**
On 23 April 2013 a single fake AP headline erased about $136.5bn of S&P 500 value, per Reuters data, and the index fell in about five seconds. On 22 May 2023 an AI-generated "Pentagon explosion" image knocked the S&P down about 0.26-0.29% within minutes. Algorithms act before a human verifies anything. GhostClock is a fast check on whether a clip was really recorded when it claims to be.

**2. "Old footage sold as new already moves prices."**
In 2008 a six-year-old bankruptcy story resurfaced as news and UAL shares fell more than 75% intraday. In May 2017 a two-week-old, misleadingly edited Comey clip played as "breaking" and trading floors reacted. Misdating needs no AI at all. GhostClock's ENF fingerprint answers exactly that question for recordings: was this captured on the date it claims?

**3. "Finance already pays for timestamp integrity, at the microsecond level."**
MiFID II RTS 25 requires HFT clocks within 100 µs of UTC and voice trades within 1 second, and MiFID II requires firms to tape trade-related calls. Regulators fined CODA Markets $90k for unsynchronised clocks, made Futures International pay $500k for pre-stamped order tickets, and took a $468m record settlement from UnitedHealth's ex-CEO for picking backdated option dates. GhostClock extends the same integrity check to the audio and video evidence (recorded calls, earnings audio, viral clips) that markets and compliance teams already rely on.

## Honesty notes for Q&A
- No case was found where **ENF itself** was used in a finance dispute. Every DIRECT/PARTIAL case is media whose time or authenticity mattered, not a case GhostClock would have solved on the record.
- Phone and VoIP audio is band-limited and usually strips mains hum. The voice-deepfake cases (#6) and recorded trader calls (#15) are weaker for ENF than room or broadcast recordings.
- The Fed "no-taper" milliseconds story (#11) is disputed (Quincy Data). Say "alleged."
- Don't use the "$500bn Pentagon swing," a "deepfake CEO crashed a stock" claim, or a "Thomson Reuters 2014 fine." None of them was verified.
