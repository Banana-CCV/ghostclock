# GhostClock: insurance case studies where evidence timing or integrity cost money

Compiled 2026-11-08 in a fast (~15 min) web pass. Every URL below was fetched in this session, and figures were checked against the fetched text, except where marked **UNVERIFIED**. Private individuals are named only when a court, police or press report names them. FOS decisions anonymise complainants, so these notes use the FOS initials.

Fit ratings:
- **DIRECT**: an audio or video recording existed and its time or integrity was the crux.
- **PARTIAL**: timing or integrity was the crux, but the evidence was photos, metadata or system logs, not A/V.
- **CONTEXT-ONLY**: statistics or background.

## Three best for a pitch slide

1. **"The CCTV clock was wrong", FOS DRN-4534311 (One Insurance, Feb 2024).** A theft claim depended on whether the car was taken before or after 22:00, because an overnight-garaging endorsement applied after that time. The claimant's own CCTV existed, but both sides accepted its clock was unreliable, so the ombudsman fell back on her early verbal statement and she lost. An independent, grid-referenced time for the footage would have turned "her word against the DVR clock" into evidence.

2. **"Eleven seconds", FOS DRN-4256057 (Wakam, Jul 2023).** An insurer declined a car-theft claim because doorbell and CCTV footage showed the car driven off 11 seconds after the thief reached it. The ombudsman upheld the complaint after an expert used the same 11 seconds to reach the opposite conclusion. When a payout hinges on a duration measured from consumer footage, both sides need assurance that the clip is continuous, unedited and at real speed. That is the splice and speed check GhostClock does.

3. **"Metadata isn't a fixed or unchangeable source", FOS DRN-3332271 (UKI, Feb 2022).** UKI branded a contents claim fraudulent based only on photo EXIF timestamps. The ombudsman overturned the decision, ordered fraud markers removed and awarded £300, noting that metadata can be edited or silently changed. The insurer lost because file metadata was its only clock. In AFCA's Youi case (Feb 2026), altered photo metadata hid a post-loss policy purchase. Investigators need a time source that sits outside the file.

Context line for the slide: UK insurers detected £1.34bn of fraudulent claims in 2025 (ABI). In the US, 99% of insurers have seen manipulated or AI-altered claim documentation, and only 58% are very confident they can spot edits to real photos or videos (Verisk, Mar 2026).

## Gaps and caveats

- No UK court judgment or FOS decision was found in which A/V footage was proven edited, sped-up or re-timestamped. The "sped-up CCTV" scenario remains anecdotal (the Reddit thread is unverified). Say so honestly in the pitch.
- In DRN-4534311 the CCTV was a home DVR. ENF can only be recovered if the recording captured mains hum (audio) or mains-driven light flicker. That is not guaranteed for CCTV without audio, so present it as "could have helped where ENF is present".
- US crash-and-buy cases are plentiful (CA DOI, Utah, Iowa, NH), but they turn on policy system logs, not recordings.
