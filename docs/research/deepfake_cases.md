# Deepfake / fabricated-media fraud: cases and figures for the GhostClock pitch

Compiled 2026-11-08. "Verified" means the page was fetched and the figure checked against it. "Search-only" means the source showed up in search results and is consistent with other sources, but the page itself would not load here (403/451/paywall). Do not quote search-only figures on a slide without opening the source first.

**GhostClock fit ratings**
- **DIRECT**: the media is a *recording* (a file submitted as evidence or proof) made indoors near mains power, and its date, time or continuity is the point in dispute. ENF can confirm or rule out the claimed time window and detect splices.
- **PARTIAL**: ENF helps with part of the case only (an archived recording of a call, or an injected/replayed pre-recorded video), or has a clear limit (live call, mobile or battery device, short clip, no mains hum).
- **CONTEXT-ONLY**: a market-size or trend number. It is useful for slides but is not something ENF analysis would have caught.

**Where ENF does not help (be upfront about this in the pitch):**
- **Live, real-time calls** (Arup, Ferrari, WPP, the UAE and UK voice clones). The fake is synthesised as the call happens, and the victim acts during the call. There is no stored file to check before the money moves. Codecs such as Opus, AMR or Teams audio also remove most hum below about 100 Hz. ENF could only help *after the fact*, on a recording of the call, and even then it would show that the call took place at that time, not that the voice was genuine.
- **Outdoor, battery-powered or mobile-phone-only recordings.** These often pick up no mains hum. Video ENF from lamp flicker needs indoor mains lighting such as fluorescent or LED drivers.
- **Very short clips.** Matching needs roughly a minute or more of usable ENF to be reliable; a clip of a few seconds is ambiguous.
- **Synthetic media with no ENF at all.** Here GhostClock can only say "no consistent grid signature", which is weak evidence because many genuine recordings also lack hum. The strong outputs are a positive date match, a mismatch, or a discontinuity.
- **Still images and documents** (insurance shallowfakes, ID-document forgeries) are out of scope.

## 2. Three best pitch summaries

**A. "A HK$200m meeting that never happened" (Arup, 2024), plus the Singapore 2025 sequel.**
A finance employee in Arup's Hong Kong office made 15 transfers totalling HK$200m (~US$25.6m) to five accounts after a video call in which the CFO and other colleagues were deepfakes built from public footage. Arup confirmed that "fake voices and images were used". In March 2025 the same playbook hit a Singapore multinational: >US$499k was sent after a deepfake Zoom call with the "CEO". It was recovered only because the fraudsters came back for another US$1.4m. Deloitte puts US genAI-enabled fraud at US$40bn by 2027, up from US$12.3bn in 2023.
*Be honest about the fit:* GhostClock does not stop a live call. Its role is as a post-incident and pre-approval control on the **recordings** that enter finance and claims workflows (meeting recordings, voice-note approvals, video KYC). It answers whether this media was captured on the grid at the claimed time and whether it is continuous, or whether it was replayed or synthesised.

**B. "The recording is the evidence": doctored and fabricated recordings in disputes.**
In a 2019 UK custody hearing, a mother submitted audio of the father making threats. It had been edited to insert words he never said, and it was exposed only because his lawyer obtained the original file and its metadata. In Baltimore (2024–25), an athletic director fabricated an AI "racist rant" recording of his principal, adding background noise to make it sound like a real room. He took a plea and got 4 months, but the principal's career was damaged. These are exactly GhostClock's DIRECT cases: an indoor recording near mains power whose *date and continuity* are the whole dispute. ENF splice detection does not need the original file, and a fabricated "room recording" with no grid signature, or the wrong one, is a red flag. The same exposure applies in finance to disputed recorded instructions, claims calls, employment tribunals and commercial disputes.

**C. "Courts already trust the grid": ENF precedent plus the scale of the problem.**
ENF is not speculative. UK Met Police forensic audio expert Dr Alan Cooper used it to rebut a defence claim that an undercover recording was spliced; the ENF matched the dates and times of the alleged deal, and three gun suppliers were convicted with 33 years combined (2012). It had already helped authenticate a disputed recording in a murder trial (2010). Today regulators are warning: FinCEN Alert FIN-2024-Alert004 (Nov 2024), a deepfake attempt every 5 minutes (Entrust, 2024), native virtual-camera injection up 2,665% (iProov, 2024), and North American deepfake fraud up 1,100% (Sumsub, Q1 2025). In those pipelines, replayed pre-recorded video is the attack (Group-IB's Indonesian case: >1,100 attempts). GhostClock makes the court-tested technique self-serve and automated for finance teams.

**Caveats for the pitch:** Do not claim GhostClock would have stopped Arup, the UAE heist or the CEO voice clones; those were live calls. Do not claim ENF detects deepfakes as such. It corroborates *when* and *whether continuously* something was recorded on the grid. Synthetic media usually *lacks* a consistent ENF, which is a signal, but absence alone is weak evidence.
